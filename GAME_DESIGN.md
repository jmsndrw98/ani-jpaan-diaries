# Ani's Japan Diaries — A Cozy Adventure with Happy & Toti
Little paws, distant places, precious memories.

Build a complete playable browser-based 2.5D isometric cozy Japanese travel game using HTML5 Canvas, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript (no framework or build step). Use the exact supplied character references: Ani (pink sakura kimono, glasses and parasol), Happy (large golden retriever, colorful samurai costume and straw hat), Toti (small tan upright-eared dog, dark brown leather armor and curled tail). Preserve their identities. The six-view sheets are references, not animation atlases. Extract clean transparent sprites, establish foot anchors, and create consistent directional walking, idle, sitting and resting frames. Never claim missing frames are complete.

Art: handcrafted chunky voxel pixel art, consistent isometric projection, pixel density, warm wood, sakura pink, muted green, lantern lighting, nearest-neighbor rendering. No generic dashboard, emoji assets or placeholder shapes in final art.

Story: opening train cinematic at sunrise; Ani opens an empty travel journal while Happy rests and Toti watches the scenery. Narration: “Japan. A place I've always dreamed of visiting. This time, I'm bringing my two favorite travel companions.” Transition into playable Kyoto.

Four distinct explorable destinations:
- Kyoto — Beneath the Sakura: traditional streets, shrine courtyard, tea shop, petals, matcha, souvenirs, first photo and passport stamp. Sunset under a cherry tree.
- Nara — The Little Wonders: forest, river, picnic, wildlife observation from a distance, hidden flower trail discovered by Toti. Rest by the river.
- Hakone — Between the Mountains: rainy ryokan, tatami room, tea, decorating, lakeside and mountain viewpoint. Rain clears at sunset.
- Tokyo — A Thousand Little Lights: lantern festival, food stalls, relaxed mini-games, souvenirs and final passport stamp. Peaceful light display.
Ending: train home, journal displays actual player photos, souvenirs and discoveries. Ani says “I thought the most beautiful part of Japan would be the places we'd visit. But it was the little moments we shared along the way.” Unlock free travel mode. All cinematics use in-game assets, camera pans, fades, parallax, dialogue and skip/replay controls.

Gameplay: eight-direction WASD/arrows, click-to-move and mobile touch, collision, pathfinding, depth sorting, camera follow, interactive objects and animated transitions. Happy calmly follows and rests; Toti explores and investigates. Both can be fed, petted, played with and have independent friendship progression. No combat, timers, forced streaks or energy restrictions.

XP: pet 5, feed 10, souvenir 15, landmark 20, activity 25, secret 50, destination 100. Prevent repetitive XP farming; unlock destinations by chapter completion, not grind. Include level rewards, accessories, achievements, travel map, train rides, passport, inventory, actual photo album and journal. Persist progress and settings locally. Compact HUD and accessible controls.

Environments: generate cohesive game-ready terrain, architecture, furniture, trees, bridges, rail station and train, festival stalls, props, UI icons and passport stamps. Animate petals, water, bamboo, lanterns, rain and tea steam. Use original or licensed audio, independent music/ambience/effects volume. Record licensing and asset metadata.

## HTML5 architecture and delivery
Use index.html, style.css and classic deferred scripts (game.js and scripts/ for movement, companions, cinematics, progression, interactions, save, audio and UI). Use Canvas 2D, requestAnimationFrame with delta time, isometric world/screen transforms, foot-anchor depth sorting and nearest-neighbor sprites. Use relative asset paths, no runtime CDN, module imports or fetch-dependent local data; use JS data objects. Support opening index.html directly and static hosting; test both. Web Audio starts only after user interaction; save with LocalStorage where available, with graceful storage failure and optional JSON save export/import. No Node, npm, Vite, TypeScript or backend required.

## Responsive and accessible gameplay
Desktop/laptop: WASD/arrows, click-to-move, keyboard interaction, adjustable camera and optional fullscreen. Tablet: tap-to-move, touch controls, pinch zoom, adaptive dialogue/inventory. Mobile: optional virtual joystick, context interaction button, collapsible HUD, readable subtitles. Support portrait and landscape without forcing rotation. Use CSS dynamic viewport units and safe-area insets, ResizeObserver or resize events, device-pixel-ratio-aware canvas backing size (cap DPR on weaker devices), camera reframing rather than stretching, minimum 44 CSS px touch targets, pointer events, and correct canvas coordinate mapping after resize. Prevent gameplay gestures from scrolling only on game surfaces; menus remain scrollable. Clear held input on blur, pointer cancel and visibility changes. Support reduced motion, mute and keyboard focus. Target 60 FPS where supported, reduce particles and DPR when necessary, pause when hidden, load destination art on demand. Test Chrome, Safari (including iOS), Firefox and Edge. Test 360x800, 390x844, 768x1024, 1024x768, 1366x768 and 1920x1080; test touch, keyboard, rotation, safe areas, journal, mini-games, save/load and cutscene skip at each device class.
